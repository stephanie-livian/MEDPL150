Reflection #1

In the opening chapter, “In Plato’s Cave”, from Susan Sontag’s book On Photography, we see the evolution of power that photography had on human beings and society as a whole throughout different centuries. Photography was used for a large range of intentions depending on one's circumstances. Good intentions being families using it to cherish memories and milestones, as well as workaholics using it on vacation to imitate the feeling that they are still working and keep a part of those experiences frozen in time forever. However, those same cameras were used for evil such as pornography and during wartime to depict gruesome images across the world, which had no bounds to whose eyes would lay on. Photographs were taken and printed around without context, leaving the people on the other end to interpret it however they saw fit. What was once taboo could easily become normalized through people's choice to photograph and publicize for everyone, young and old, to see. To me, photography is a positive way of capturing moments that make me happy to look back on and remind me of small details that may have slipped my memory. At times that could mean happy occasions with friends and family so that I can remember who I was with, or our inside jokes we couldn't stop laughing about. It could also be physical things that brought me joy, like a fresh manicure or clothing that I saw in a Fifth Avenue window. Lastly, visually aesthetic shots that show the beauty of this world such as bright sunsets, and the beach with a clear blue sky and calm water. Looking back through my camera roll, those images that I once took without thinking twice remind me of moments where I was so happy and I did not want them to end. Another area where photography plays an important role for me is through my job with jewelry. Although it is through my iphone, I use the same knowledge of taking pictures to capture white-background images of fine jewelry. Something which seems so simple, yet is actually so intricate and important in a different sense. The way that the different colored stones are picked up with a camera affects the way that a client will portray that piece through the other end of the screen. The image of a piece needs to be captured and retouched in a way that will show the value and quality of its craftsmanship. This is completely different than a person taking an image of jewelry ‘just because’. Through this experience I have learned that the same image can easily be taken differently based on the photographic intent a person has prior to capturing an image. Photography could have great power depending on who it reaches and how they perceive it. Notions of our world at this moment can be represented through photographs posted on the internet or in newspapers. What one may think is insignificant can be tremendously important for someone else. When printed in newspapers, the same image can be viewed in countless ways - not including the context of the image. In our day and age it has become very accessible to snap a quick image and post it online, without even noticing what may be in the background. In every form of photography, we see that it could affect a broader crowd with a range of reactions.

Reflection #2

In the past few years we have seen the dramatic increase in the use of AI around society. Our technology, our healthcare, our workspaces, and so much more; all infiltrated by artificial intelligence. Whether this change has been for the good or the bad is very controversial and debatable. It is also very subjective. For some, it has taken away their jobs, and for others it is a tool they use to get through school or work. However, one thing we know for certain is that AI definitely has its pros and cons, as does every new innovation in this world. The purposes it is used for and the extent to which it is used highly contributes to this debate. There are many AI platforms and their levels of accuracy differ, and this plays another factor on the topic. Throughout the years, such platforms have continuously grown and their quality of AI has improved. With time, the use of AI has only become more and more normalized across generations and different careers. Now, everyone has it at the tip of their fingertips to use at their will. In the passage “Your Brain on ChatGPT”, we learn about a study taken to assess the cognitive effects of the use of AI. The participants were divided into three groups to write an essay. The first group was allowed to use AI, the second group was allowed to use a search engine, and the last group was only allowed to use their brains. The results clearly show us what many people already believe regarding the negative effects of using AI (rather than your brain for example). By studying the participants' brain activity, it is proved that the brain-only group had the strongest cognitive engagement, the search engine group had mild engagement, and the AI using group had the weakest engagement. Not only during the use of AI did it affect their neural activity, but even afterwards when going back to using their brains, it remained weaker than the other groups. From this we can fully understand how much the use of AI impacts and alters the natural way our brain is wired. The use of AI can be very helpful to many people in different lines of work, but it can also be quite harmful. The easy accessibility of it to anyone and everyone makes that harm much harder to avoid. Although at times it can be acceptable to use, as a society we must be careful that it does not take over us using our own brains to do things. Whether it is used for school work, office work, or even using it for a side hobby as a creative outlet, it can always have its advantages and disadvantages. We must also keep in mind that whatever we use it for, will hold a different value than something we do or create ourselves.

Reflection #3

In the graphic novel, Motherhood in crisis, we see the tragic stories of various women from Sierra Leone, depicted through comic illustration. The story I’d like to focus on is Heela’s, a schoolgirl who got raped and had her education and future career stolen from her. I believe that illustration was used for these stories in order to make certain parts easier to look at for its viewers. For instance, the depiction of Heela getting raped and of Tetteh being physically abused while pregnant. This helps us as viewers to watch and learn about these stories although the slides do become gruesome and hard to look at. The illustrations are displayed like instagram stories, which I think makes it more relatable for our generation to “tap through” and view. There are also speech bubbles on some slides that help make the stories feel more realistic. It gives the feeling that we’re watching the women's stories unfold in real time. The speech bubbles also add another dimension to the illustrations which makes them more interesting. An additional reason for using illustration could be that there simply weren't photographs and videos of all the moments described in the women’s stories. The women told their stories months and years after they occurred and they probably had little to no footage from throughout their experiences. Therefore, I think these illustrations make it a great way for the world to remember what happened to these women and to help spread their stories. The illustrations vary from the photographs visually as they are less graphic. They protect our eyes in a sense as we do not see the atmosphere around Sierra Leone. The comic illustrations prevent us from seeing the realness of what it looks like, such as the dirt on the floor, cracks on the wall, or even perhaps the people ripped clothing. On one hand, I think this can be good as it is censoring the content in a way, since one may never know whos eyes these photographs can reach. However, on the other hand, I think that it is stopping us from fully understanding the seriousness of these illustrations. One advantage the illustrations present in comparison to text only, is that they help us envision what these women went through. Whereas, if it were text only it would be left to our imagination and how far we are able and willing to go with our imagination. In my opinion, the illustrations act as a bridge between text and photography and they meet us halfway to tell the stories. Although, I also believe that it is important that they used photography and videos at the end of their “instagram stories” to make sure we understand how real these stories are, and that it's not just a comic book.
